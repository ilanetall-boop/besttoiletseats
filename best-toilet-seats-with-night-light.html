Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Night light toilet seats use a small LED strip or ring built into the seat or hinge area, so you don't need to install a separate plug-in light.
  • Motion-activated models turn the light on automatically when you approach, then shut off after a set time to save battery.
  • Hinge type matters as much as the light itself: quick-release and soft-close hinges make cleaning and daily use noticeably easier.
  • Bidet-style night light seats such as the KERDE P30B and WLJBIDET cost more but add heated seating and a water wash function beyond illumination.
  • Elongated and round seats aren't interchangeable, so measure your existing seat before ordering.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do toilet seat night lights need batteries?

Most night light toilet seats use replaceable AA or AAA batteries housed near the hinge, since running a wire to a bathroom outlet for a small LED alone usually isn't practical. Electronic bidet seats with a night light, like the KERDE P30B and WLJBIDET on this list, plug into a nearby outlet instead and power the light from the same connection as the wash and heating functions.

Q: Will a night light toilet seat fit my current toilet?

It depends on your bowl shape. Elongated bowls, the most common shape in US homes built after the 1990s, measure roughly 18.5 inches from the mounting bolts to the front edge. Round bowls measure closer to 16.5 inches. Most seats in this guide are elongated only, and the KOHLER Cachet Nightlight Round Toilet is the one built specifically for round bowls, so measure your existing seat before ordering.

Q: Are night light toilet seats worth the extra cost over a plain seat?

For a bathroom that gets visited overnight, especially by kids or older adults, a built-in night light removes the need to flip on a bright overhead light or install a separate plug-in fixture. The Lumiere and KOHLER Rutledge options on this list add the feature for well under ten dollars more than a comparable plain seat, which makes it a low-cost upgrade for households with someone who gets up at night.
