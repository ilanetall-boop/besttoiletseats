Key Differences Explained

1. Size and Dimensions

The most obvious difference is length. Elongated seats measure approximately 18.5 inches from the bolt holes to the front edge, while round seats measure about 16.5 inches. That 2-inch difference significantly impacts comfort and bathroom layout.

Width is nearly identical at around 14 inches. Bolt spacing is also the same (5.5 inches center-to-center), meaning mounting hardware is interchangeable. However, never put an elongated seat on a round bowl or vice versa — the seat must match the bowl shape for safety.

Pro Tip: Not sure which shape you have? Measure from the bolt holes to the front of the bowl. Under 17" = round. Over 17" = elongated. See our complete toilet seat guide for more details.

2. Comfort

Most adults prefer elongated seats for everyday comfort. The extra 2 inches provides better thigh support, especially for taller individuals. The oval shape also gives more room in the front, which many users find more hygienic during longer periods of sitting.

Round seats work perfectly well for average-sized adults and are often preferred for children's bathrooms. For families with small children, consider a dedicated potty training seat that fits on either shape.

3. Space Requirements

Round toilets project about 25-28 inches from the wall, while elongated toilets need 28-31 inches. If your bathroom is tight on space, those 2-3 extra inches can make the difference between comfort and cramped knees.

Building codes typically require at least 15 inches from the toilet centerline to any side wall, and 21 inches of clearance in front. In a small bathroom, a round toilet helps you meet these minimums more easily.

4. Price

Round and elongated seats from the same brand typically cost about the same. The KOHLER Brevia Elongated at $30.81 is actually less expensive than the KOHLER Cachet Round at $48.59 — though the Cachet has the premium ReadyLatch system.

Budget seats in both shapes start around $15-20. Premium options with soft-close hinges range from $30-75, and bidet seats can run $200-500+.

5. Availability

Elongated toilets dominate new construction, accounting for roughly 70% of residential sales. Round toilets are still common in older homes, apartments, and powder rooms. All major brands offer both shapes in their full product lines.

6. ADA Compliance

The ADA recommends elongated bowls at 17-19 inches seat height for accessibility. If you or a family member has mobility challenges, an elongated toilet paired with a raised toilet seat provides the most accessible setup.

How to Measure Your Toilet

Before buying a new toilet seat, confirm your bowl shape:

  1. Find the bolt holes: Lift your current seat and locate the two bolts at the back of the bowl.
  2. Measure to the front: Measure from the center point between the bolt holes to the front outer edge.
  3. Read: 16.5" or less = Round. 18-18.5" = Elongated.
  4. Check bolt spacing: Standard is 5.5 inches center-to-center.
Important: Some brands use proprietary bolt spacing. Always check seat compatibility before ordering.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between elongated and round toilet seats?

Elongated seats are about 2 inches longer (18.5" vs 16.5"), providing more seating area. Round seats are more compact and better for small bathrooms. Both use the same bolt spacing.

Can I put an elongated seat on a round toilet?

No. The seat will overhang the front by about 2 inches, creating an unstable and unsafe seat. Always match seat shape to bowl shape.

Which shape is more comfortable?

Most adults find elongated seats more comfortable due to the extra length and better thigh support. However, round seats work perfectly well for most people.

Are elongated or round more popular?

Elongated toilets account for roughly 70% of residential sales and are standard in new construction. Round remains popular in older homes and small bathrooms.

How do I know if my toilet is elongated or round?

Measure from the bolt holes to the front edge. Under 17 inches = round. Over 17 inches = elongated.

Do elongated toilets take up more space?

Yes. Elongated toilets project 28-31 inches from the wall vs 25-28 inches for round. Those 2-3 extra inches matter in small bathrooms.
