The Mayfair Collins Slow Close Toilet Seat is an elongated replacement seat priced at $44.09, aimed at buyers tired of lids slamming shut. Its slow-close hinge lowers the lid gradually instead of letting gravity take over, which matters most in households with kids, older adults, or anyone sharing a bathroom at odd hours. The seat combines a wood core with a plastic finish, a construction choice that trades some weight savings for the sturdier feel wood-core seats are known for.

What We Liked

The clearest strength of the Mayfair Collins Slow Close Toilet Seat is the hinge itself. Owners report that the slow-close mechanism keeps working months after installation, which is not something every budget seat manages once the spring or cam inside wears down. A slow-close hinge that fails after a year of use effectively turns into a regular hinge, and the lid is back to slamming.

The elongated shape helps too, since it fits most two piece toilets sold in the US. Reviewers also point to the plastic-and-wood construction as sturdier under weight than the thinner, all-plastic seats some competitors sell at a similar price. For buyers replacing an older Mayfair or Bemis seat, the Collins keeps a familiar mounting pattern, which reviewers note makes installation straightforward without extra hardware.

Brand continuity matters more than it might seem for a product this simple. A toilet seat gets opened and closed dozens of times a day, and small hinge tolerances compound over years rather than weeks. Buyers who already own a Mayfair seat and are satisfied with how it aged tend to stick with the brand for that reason, and the Collins gives them a slow-close option without relearning how the hinges mount.

What Could Be Better

Price is the most obvious drawback, even if it is a small one. At $44.09, the Mayfair Collins Slow Close Toilet Seat costs more than the Church 295SSCT, Carex E-Z Lock, and Mayfair Bennett alternatives covered in this review, and the gap is hard to justify unless the slow-close hinge is a priority. The wood-and-plastic build, while sturdy, also adds weight compared to solid plastic seats, which can make the lid feel heavier to lift than buyers expect coming from a basic seat.

The seat only fits elongated bowls, so anyone with a round toilet needs to look elsewhere, Mayfair included. We also found less publicly available data on hinge cycle ratings or long-term load testing than we would like, so some of the durability claims here rest on buyer feedback rather than manufacturer specs. That is worth knowing before you spend more on a hinge upgrade than you would on a basic replacement seat.

None of this makes the Mayfair Collins Slow Close Toilet Seat a bad purchase. It means the extra money buys a specific tradeoff, quieter closing and Mayfair's mounting hardware, rather than a seat that is objectively better across every category. Buyers who do not value those two things specifically are better served by one of the three alternatives below.

Who Is It For?

The Mayfair Collins Slow Close Toilet Seat makes the most sense for households that have already decided a slow-close hinge is worth paying for, whether that is because of young kids, a shared bathroom, or wanting fewer slammed lids late at night. It also fits buyers replacing an existing Mayfair or Bemis seat who want a familiar mounting pattern and brand reliability rather than shopping around.

If you need a raised seat for easier sitting and standing, or a commercial-grade open front design, this is not your seat. The Carex E-Z Lock and Church 295SSCT alternatives below cover those needs directly, and neither asks you to pay extra for a slow-close hinge you may not need.
